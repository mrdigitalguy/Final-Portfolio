Product context

This site sells the rental model, not the campus one

driEV runs two business models. The website serves the public Rental model, with fixed plans. The campus Community model lives in a separate native app with its own pricing — covered in the app case study. I designed the website in parallel with that app.

The problem

Customers couldn’t find driEV, price it or book it

The company had a public rental model with daily, weekly, monthly and yearly plans, but no web presence. Everything went through WhatsApp.

  • No searchable presence
  • No pricing page and no trust signals
  • Booking required a WhatsApp conversation
  • Prospects dropped off before completing a booking
Research

I drew on interviews from the community app and benchmarked Indian EV and vehicle rental sites. Three points kept people from booking.

Drop-off 1Unclear pricing
Drop-off 2Unclear booking process
Drop-off 3Unclear steps after payment

Two more things shaped the design. Visitors worried about running out of charge, which is specific to EVs. And most would find driEV on their phone — through Instagram, WhatsApp or Google — so mobile came first.

Design principle

Answer the next question before the user has to ask it

The aim was to make booking the only logical next step. I mapped the pages before designing any screen.

Key design decisions

Three choices that shaped the site

Pricing on the homepage

Pricing sits on the homepage, not behind a quote form. Plan cards are built to scan in about five seconds.

Four steps, not eight to twelve

Competitors used eight to twelve steps. Here, documents are submitted later, so the first booking stays short.

Mobile first

Designed on a 390px frame, then scaled up to desktop. On mobile, a sticky “Book now” bar stays within reach.

The booking flow

Four steps, designed to finish in under two minutes on mobile

  1. 01Plan

    Choose daily, weekly, monthly or yearly.

  2. 02Date

    Pick the start date and add contact details.

  3. 03Pick-up

    Collect from the warehouse or get home delivery.

  4. 04Confirm

    Review and confirm. Documents come later.

What launched
  • The business can now be found online
  • WhatsApp is out of the conversion path
  • Bookings are recorded in a system and can be traced
  • The website and the community app share one brand
What I learned

Trust comes from clear answers, not more pages.

Price, process and what happens after payment were the three doubts that stopped bookings. Putting each in plain view, and keeping the flow short, did more than any visual flourish. The community app’s interviews fed straight into this site.
